Explore how the world regulates artificial light at night, safeguards biodiversity and protects dark skies. Each legal record links to its original sources.

Read the figures with care. The atlas covers 193 UN member states, two UN observers, two freely associated states, Taiwan and 31 non-sovereign territories. Svalbard and Jan Mayen share one ISO statistical entry but have different legal frameworks. Documented lighting obligations are not all dedicated light-pollution laws, and their current enforceability may require verification.

How the legal evidence is classified

Dedicated legislation specifically regulates light pollution. Sectoral and subnational regulations apply to defined activities or places. General legal frameworks may address unwanted artificial light without setting general lighting limits. Guidance, technical standards and voluntary certifications are not automatically binding. Pending documentation does not establish that no law exists.

Environmental impact assessments, proposed regulations, permit conditions and enacted laws are distinguished by legal force, territorial scope and date. The atlas is a research resource, not legal advice.
